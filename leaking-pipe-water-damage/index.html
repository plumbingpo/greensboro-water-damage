Dealing With Leaking Pipe Water Damage in Greensboro

Most pipe leaks start without any warning. The drip is hidden in a wall cavity, a sink cabinet or beneath the slab, so the first clue tends to be a stain, a spongy patch of floor or an oddly high water bill. Often that means weeks of moisture have already gone into whatever surrounds the pipe. We serve Greensboro homeowners by tracing the full wet area and drying every bit of it, including what is out of sight.

The plumbing repair stops the water, but it does not remove what has already soaked in. Getting it out is the work we do. With meters and a thermal camera we locate every wet material, make only the openings required, then keep drying equipment running until moisture levels read normal again. Because slow leaks give mold time to start, we also check for growth and treat affected surfaces.

Do This First

Before We Arrive

  1. Shut Off the WaterFor a leak at a toilet or sink, turn off the small supply valve feeding that fixture. Not sure where it is coming from? Close the main shutoff for the whole house.
  2. Stay Clear of ElectricityIf water is near outlets, switches or a light fixture, switch off the breaker for that area. Keep your hands off cords and devices that have gotten wet.
  3. Read the MeterWith every tap off, look at the meter. Movement on the dial means water is escaping out of sight, perhaps beneath the slab.
  4. Empty and PhotographBefore a repair is made or anything goes in the trash, remove what is stored near the leak and get pictures of the pipe, the staining and the warping.
  5. Get a Moisture CheckBefore the opening is sealed, get the surrounding materials checked with a moisture meter. Inside the cavity, moisture can remain even when the wall surface feels dry.
Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

A slow leak and a sudden one are not handled the same way by insurance. Sudden and accidental damage is often covered, but damage from a leak that went on for weeks or months usually is not, and some policies exclude it by name. If the leak was concealed and you could not have known, it may be viewed more favorably than a visible leak nobody dealt with. Check your policy and ask your agent. Our team records where the leak came from, along with photos and moisture readings, to give the adjuster clear facts.

Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • Fasteners from remodeling work or wall hangings that nick or pierce a line inside the wall.
  • Cold snaps that freeze piping in unheated spaces, leaving small cracks that leak after the thaw.
  • Years of excessive water pressure straining valves and joints until one of them starts to weep.
  • Failed solder joints or crimp rings hidden inside wall and ceiling cavities.
  • Soil movement and rubbing that wear a hole in a line buried below the slab.

What the Water Damages

Damaged Flooring

A leak in a wall or under the slab sends water beneath the floor covering. Hardwood cups, laminate swells at the seams, and adhesive under vinyl and tile lets go.

Soaked Wall Insulation

Insulation in the wall soaks up leak water like a sponge and holds it against the framing. Since a closed wall gives it almost no airflow, soaked insulation is normally removed.

Hidden Mold Growth

Nothing suits mold better than the dark, moist space inside a wall. During a slow leak, the back side of the drywall can be covered well before the painted face shows one spot.

Swollen Cabinets

Particleboard cabinet bases under a sink swell and crumble once they absorb water. Swollen board stays swollen, and after a lengthy leak the base often has to be replaced.

Our Process

How We Handle Leaking Pipe Water Damage

1

Find Every Wet Area

Using a thermal camera and moisture meters, our crew checks cabinets, floors and walls and marks each wet spot, even those far from the pipe that water reached by running along the framing.

2

Open What Is Needed

Small, planned openings give us access to wet cavities. Insulation, drywall or cabinet pieces beyond saving come out, and moldy material is bagged to keep spores from spreading through the house.

3

Structural Drying

We keep dehumidifiers and air movers going until the subfloor and framing read normal, which usually takes 3 to 5 days. We check readings on each visit and adjust the equipment as materials dry.

4

Treat and Document

Affected surfaces get an antimicrobial treatment, and you receive photos along with the moisture logs. The records can help with a claim, and they confirm everything was dry before it was rebuilt.
